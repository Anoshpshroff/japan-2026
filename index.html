Critical · 26 Oct

The Miyako → Kakunodate day is still long

NH1080 lands Tokyo 14:25; you are on a Shinkansen by about 16:20 and into Morioka around 18:35. Dinner in Morioka, cars at 20:00 as booked, then 1h 20m on Route 46 to the inn — in around 21:30. Plan nothing else for the evening, and treat the rental desk’s closing time as the fixed point of the day.

Solved · 26 Oct

Morioka pickup fixes the car problem

Getting off the Shinkansen at Morioka rather than riding to Kakunodate means you collect both cars the same night. You are in around 18:35 and the pickup is booked for 20:00, so dinner comes first and the cars after. That frees the 27th morning entirely — you can be at Dakigaeri at first light. But 20:00 is the end of that desk’s day and the booking is the last slot in it, so it is the hard deadline of the whole trip: ring 019-622-0100 from Haneda if the flight slips. Toyota holds a reservation an hour past the slot, but only if you call.

Critical · 2 Nov

Leave Aomori at 07:00 on the 2nd

You are driving the cars back to Morioka and taking the Shinkansen from there — no one-way drop fee, and ¥2,660 cheaper each. That means keeping the cars overnight on the 1st and being on the road by 07:00, at Morioka by 09:15, and Narita by about 13:55. The Seattle flight at 17:25 is the one that sets the clock, not the 18:25.

Good news · 28 Oct

Akita-Komagatake is open — and you can drive up

Semboku City's page is unambiguous: the prefectural Komagatake Line closes for winter from 1 November to 31 May, so the 28th is inside the season. The private-vehicle restriction only runs to 20 October, so you drive yourselves up to the 8th Station at ~1,300 m. Summit snow is the one variable.

Safety · 24–26 Oct

No-fly window after diving

You fly at 11:50 on the 26th, and the standard surface interval after several days of repetitive diving is 18 hours minimum. Finish diving on the 24th and keep the 25th snorkel-and-beach only.

Timing · 31 Oct

Osorezan closes for the season on 31 October

If the sulphur-and-ash netherworld on Shimokita appeals, 30 or 31 October is your only window — shut from 1 November until May. A 2h+ drive each way, so it's a whole day, and it competes with Hirosaki.

Logistics · 21 Oct

You land at Narita but leave from Haneda

Easy to overlook. JL933 to Miyakojima goes from HND, not NRT. Akasaka to Haneda is 35–45 minutes on the Toei Asakusa line via Sengakuji — leave the hotel by 08:15.

Daylight · Tohoku

Sunset is around 16:35 up north

By late October Aomori loses the light shortly after 16:30, with about ten and a half hours of daylight. Gorge walks and ropeways must start in the morning — the reason the northern legs are paced as they are.

Kit · Miyakojima

Water is 27 °C but cooling

Late October sits just after the typhoon season tails off — air ~28 °C, sea ~27 °C. But the sea cools from mid-month and you're doing three days of repetitive diving. A 3 mm full suit is right, 5 mm if you feel the cold.

Why late October is the right time

Fuji is famously shy — it hides behind cloud most of the summer. Visibility climbs steeply through autumn as the humid air clears, and late October into winter is the most reliable window of the year. The summit usually takes its first snow in early October, so you get the postcard version. Mornings are markedly better than afternoons: cloud builds around the peak as the day warms.

Decide on the morning

This is the one day worth checking before you commit. There are live webcams pointed at the mountain from Kawaguchiko, and the forecast is usually honest about cloud. Worth knowing

The climb

Chureito's 398 steps

They are steep and they are the reason some people skip it. There is a gently sloped path from the same car park that gets to the same viewpoint without a single step — ask for the "slope route". Worth knowing before anyone talks themselves out of it.

Timing

Morning beats afternoon

Cloud builds around the summit as the ground warms, so the mountain is most reliably clear early. That is the argument for the 08:30 train rather than a leisurely start — and for doing Chureito first, before the light flattens.

Aim to be at the pagoda by 11:00

Autumn

You are just before the peak

The Momiji Corridor's maple festival on the lake's north-west shore normally runs from early November, so late October catches it turning rather than fully lit. The kokia at Oishi Park are red now, though, and the higher slopes have colour.

Colour rises with altitude

Eat

Hoto, and Fujizakura beer

Hoto is the dish to have — nothing like udon despite appearances, and exactly right after a cold morning at the lake. The local Fujizakura Kogen brewery does a very good weizen if anyone wants a beer with the view.

Group of six

Buses versus a charter

The sightseeing buses are frequent and cheap but they run to a timetable, and Chureito plus Oishi Park in one day means watching the clock. A private charter for six removes that entirely and often costs little more once you total six rail fares.

Worth pricing before you book

The one rule that matters

Do Fushimi Inari first, before 09:00. Everything else in this plan tolerates crowds; the torii tunnels do not. The photograph everyone wants — an empty corridor of vermilion gates — exists for about ninety minutes a day, and you are on the only train that gets you there in time.

Worth knowing

Etiquette

Geiko are not an attraction

If you see a geiko or maiko hurrying between appointments in Gion, do not follow or photograph them. Kyoto has posted rules and fines on the private lanes off Hanamikoji after years of harassment. Look, step aside, let them pass.

Private alleys are signposted

Timing

You are three weeks early

Kyoto's maples turn from about the middle of November and peak late in the month. In late October the city is green with the first hints of colour — so you get Kyoto's architecture and streets, but not the thing it is most famous for. Plan accordingly and you will not be disappointed.

Crowds, however, are already here

Feet

Twelve to fifteen kilometres

Fushimi Inari's approach is stairs, Kiyomizu is a hill, and Higashiyama is cobbles the whole way down. This is comfortably the most walking of any day on the trip. Real shoes, and be honest about whether both parents want it on day three.

The Tokyo track exists for exactly this

Money

Where the ¥28k goes

Roughly ¥14,000 each way on Nozomi, and it is unavoidable if you want nine hours in the city. Temple entries are small by comparison — a few hundred yen each. For six people the transport alone is about ¥170,000, which is worth stating plainly before anyone commits.

Fuji is roughly a fifth of this

Eat

Yudofu and obanzai

Kyoto's cooking is vegetable-led and restrained — yudofu (tofu simmered at the table in kombu broth) and obanzai (small seasonal home-style dishes) are the two things worth seeking out. Nishiki Market is the browsing option if the schedule slips.

Higashiyama has the best yudofu houses

The dive briefing

All Open Water · 22–24 Oct

Miyako isn't a coral-garden destination — it's a limestone maze of arches, tunnels and grottoes cut through the rock off Shimoji-jima, and that terrain is what people fly in for. The catch is depth: all six of you are Open Water, which caps you at 18 m, and the three sites Miyako is famous for all sit below that line. This section is built around that rather than around pretending otherwise.

Where 18 metres leaves you

Devil's Palace bottoms out around 25 m. Antonio Gaudí is 30 m+. Toriike runs past 40 m with cave ceilings at 23 m. None of the three is an Open Water dive, and no honest shop will take you to them on an OW card. Toriike in particular is a genuine overhead cave that operators gate behind 50 logged dives — that one is off the table this trip whatever you do.

The fix, and it fits your week exactly

You said you don't mind going a little deeper. Aquatic Adventure teaches the NAUI Advanced course themselves — ¥49,500, four dives over two days, certifying you to 30 m. Run it on the 22nd and 23rd and the 24th becomes a fun-dive day with Devil's Palace within reach. Your three dive days were already blocked out; this only changes what they contain. Other agencies' Open Water cards are accepted as the prerequisite, so your existing cards work.

If nobody fancies a course

Entirely reasonable, and Miyako still delivers. The shallow, sunlit end of the cave menu is the same landscape with light coming in and an exit in sight: Nakanoshima Channel, Twin Caves, Mini Toriike and the Wonderful Cave grottoes. BIGHOLIDAY's own difficulty guide rates these "elementary to intermediate — shallow and lets in plenty of light". Add Yabiji reef for a colour-and-fish day and you have three strong days inside 18 m.

Eating on Miyakojima

Hirara is the only real nightlife

Okinawan food is its own cuisine, not a regional variant of Japanese — pork-heavy, subtropical, and with a distinct island vocabulary. Restaurants outside Hirara close early, so plan dinners rather than wandering.

The island dish

Miyako soba

宮古そば

Thick, udon-like wheat noodles in a lighter, ramen-ish broth under fall-apart stewed pork ribs — and traditionally the meat is hidden under the noodles, a local quirk. The famous shops close when the pot runs out, so this is really a lunch dish. Koja Soba is the long-standing name.

Go by 11:00 · 25 Oct is your window

The big night

Miyako beef

宮古牛

Rarer than Ishigaki beef and barely leaves the island. Have it as yakiniku, as seared nigiri, or in the beef yakisoba the izakayas do particularly well. This is the 24th's group dinner — book it.

24 Oct · reserve ahead

Order it everywhere

Umibudo & the island plate

海ぶどう

"Sea grapes" — a seaweed of tiny green spheres that burst with salt water like caviar, served with ponzu. Alongside: goya champuru (bitter melon stir-fried with tofu, egg and pork), rafute (belly pork simmered in awamori), and gurukun, the Okinawan prefectural fish, deep-fried whole.

The local spirit

Awamori

泡盛

Okinawa's rice spirit — distilled rather than brewed, made with long-grain Thai rice and black koji, and older than shochu. Miyako has its own distilleries: Taragawa, Chuko and Ikema. Ask for kūsu, aged three years or more, which is far smoother. Sort your driver out first.

Distillery visits on the 25th

The right first night

三線

The sanshin is Okinawa's three-stringed snakeskin banjo and the ancestor of the shamisen. Several Hirara izakayas run live sets nightly, usually ending with everyone up dancing kachāshī. It is not a tourist performance — it's how the island socialises. Book, especially in season.

Nothing like the Route 46 crossing you did on the 26th. The one variable is early-November snow on the higher ground around Nishine, which is what the winter tyres on your booking list are for.

Oirase Gorge

奥入瀬渓流

Fourteen kilometres of stream running out of Lake Towada past moss, rapids and a dozen named waterfalls, on a level path the whole way. Walk any length you like. With two cars, drop one carload at Nenokuchi and collect them at Ishigedo — the classic middle section, and the best use of having a driver.

Tsuta-numa pond loop

蔦沼

Six ponds linked by a level path through old beech forest. Tsuta-numa itself produces the mirror reflection that is one of the most photographed autumn scenes in Japan — and it's only glass early, before the wind gets up. Worth an early start.

Easy · 60–90 min

Hakkoda summit boardwalk

八甲田

Straight off the ropeway at 1,324 m: a marked, level boardwalk loop across the alpine plateau with views over the whole range. No navigation, no scrambling — but exposed and genuinely cold by late October, so proper layers.

Easy · ~40 min

Mt Iwaki via the Skyline

岩木山 · 1,625 m

Drive 69 numbered hairpins to the 8th station, take a chairlift to the 9th, then walk the last rocky stretch to the summit of the mountain that dominates every view in Tsugaru. The biggest payoff-to-effort ratio in the region. Road closes early November.

Easy · 1–2 h

Juniko & Aoike

十二湖・青池

Thirty-three lakes (despite the name) scattered through UNESCO beech forest. Aoike is the reason to come — a small pond of impossible glowing cobalt whose colour still isn't fully explained. Level paths link the main lakes.

31 Oct · 2h drive each way

Moderate · ~3 h

Anmon Falls

暗門の滝

The real Shirakami walk: a river-gorge trail with boardwalks and crossings leading to three stacked waterfalls, through the largest virgin beech forest in East Asia. Wet underfoot and gear is sometimes required — confirm the trail's open this late.

31 Oct · for the active four

Hard · 4–5 h

Mt Odake

大岳 · 1,585 m

The proper Hakkoda traverse, from the ropeway over the high ground and down past the sulphur vents at Jigoku-numa to Sukayu Onsen. Superb, but snow is likely by 30 October and it turns into a winter mountain fast — check conditions and be willing to turn back.

The classic

Hakkoda–Towada Gold Line

国道103号

Aomori city up over the Hakkoda range past Sukayu and Jigoku-numa, down through Tsuta and along Oirase to Lake Towada. One of the most celebrated autumn drives in Japan and the backbone of both your 29th and your 30th.

~2h without stops · you will stop

Coastal

Route 101, the Nihonkai coast

日本海沿い

Empty coastal highway along the Sea of Japan towards Shirakami, with the wave-cut rock shelf at Senjojiki and long unbroken views west. Best driven late, heading home with the sun going down over the water.

31 Oct · the approach to Juniko

Indoor · 1 h

Nebuta Warasse

ねぶたの家 ワ・ラッセ

Five of the previous year's prize-winning Nebuta floats, indoors and lit, at close range — twenty-odd feet of painted washi warriors on wire frames. The festival itself is August, but this is genuinely astonishing year-round, and it's beside the station.

Any evening · 5 min from the hotel

Indoor + outdoor · 2 h

Sannai-Maruyama & the art museum

三内丸山遺跡

A UNESCO-listed Jomon village occupied for over a millennium, with reconstructed pit dwellings and a six-pillar timber tower. Next door, the Aomori Museum of Art holds Nara's giant Aomori-ken dog and Chagall's Aleko backdrops. The best bad-weather day in the prefecture.

1 Nov · 20 min from the hotel

Baths worth the drive

Sukayu, Tsuta & Aoni

酸ヶ湯・蔦・青荷

Sukayu's "thousand-person bath" is a vast all-hinoki hall, mixed and famously acidic. Tsuta's cypress tub has water rising through the floorboards. Aoni, up a mountain road near Kuroishi, has no electricity by design and is lit entirely by oil lamps — day bathing available and worth arranging.

Osorezan

恐山

One of the three holiest sites in Japan: an ash-grey volcanic crater of steaming vents and stone cairns beside a startlingly blue caldera lake, understood as the entrance to the underworld. Free onsen huts on the temple grounds. Two-plus hours each way, and the season ends on 31 October.

Apple picking at Hirosaki

りんご狩り

Aomori grows more than half of Japan’s apples, and Hirosaki Apple Park runs picking until mid-November — which puts you squarely in Fuji season, the best of them and the last to ripen. Eighty varieties, a few hundred yen, and it slots straight into the 31st if you take the Hirosaki option.

Pairs with the castle and the cider

Nothing like you expect

Tsugaru-jamisen, live and close

津軽三味線

Forget the polite image of shamisen — Tsugaru style is percussive, furiously fast, and played a metre from your table. Hirosaki has dedicated venues (Anzu, Aiya, Adohadari) where you eat Tsugaru food while it happens. The single most memorable evening available on this leg, and most people never think to do it.

Book a table; the rooms are small

Beside the station

A-Factory cidre

シードル工房

A glass-walled cider works in the harbour building, so you watch it being made and then drink it. Kimori and the house flight run dry to sweet, all from Aomori apples. Five minutes from the hotel, open late, and the best possible use of a spare hour before dinner.

Also the best place to buy apple everything

Prize-winning floats from past festivals kept indoors year-round — enormous, lit from within, genuinely startling at close range. But the part people miss is that you can beat the taiko and be taught the haneto jump yourself. Ten minutes of that beats an hour of reading captions.

Eating in Aomori

Scallop season is just opening

Cold water on three sides and the best apple land in Japan behind. Everything here is either pulled out of Mutsu Bay that morning or grown within an hour's drive.

Breakfast · the one to do

Nokkedon

のっけ丼

At Furukawa market: buy a bowl of rice and a book of tickets, then walk the stalls trading tickets for whatever you want on top — scallop, uni, tuna, salmon roe, squid. You build the bowl yourself, which is most of the pleasure. Mornings only; check the weekly closing day.

1 Nov · 5 min from the hotel

Mutsu Bay

Hotate & kaiyaki-miso

帆立・貝焼き味噌

Aomori grows more scallops than anywhere in Japan and the season starts now. Best as kaiyaki-miso — scallop and egg cooked with miso in a big shell used as the pan, a Tsugaru fishermen's dish. Sweet, rich, and served everywhere from markets to izakayas.

1 Nov · last dinner

Local rarity

Ichigo-ni

いちご煮

Sea urchin and abalone in a clear dashi, named because the urchin suspended in the cloudy broth looks like wild strawberries. A Hachinohe festival dish and one of the more expensive things you'll eat — worth ordering once, properly, on the last night.

1 Nov · order it once

Aomori grows over half of Japan's apples, centred on Hirosaki, and late October is harvest. The tourist office maps around fifty distinct local apple pies. Then there's the hard cider poured at A-Factory on the Aomori waterfront, with a tasting flight of four.

せんべい汁

Wheat crackers made specifically to be boiled, broken into a chicken or pork broth with vegetables where they go chewy rather than soggy. A frugal Nambu dish turned regional icon, and exactly what you want after a cold day at Hakkoda.

Seven days, and not one more

Yamato lets you designate a delivery date only within seven days of dispatch. Ship on the 25th and 1 November is the last date you can name — which happens to be exactly the one you want. There is no slack in this. Ship a day late and the only date left is the 2nd, and the service requires arrival before your flight day, not on it.

Confirm the date is accepted when you book

Stays with you

What rides in hand luggage on the 26th

Just the one day’s worth: one warm layer each, because you leave Miyakojima at 26 °C and stand in Morioka at about 8 °C the same evening, and your coats are already at the inn. Plus every passport, all cash and cards, medication, and anything expensive — dive computers especially. Compensation caps at ¥300,000 a parcel, and you cannot open a case that is in Chiba.

Coats are at the inn; the island cases are at Narita

Before you seal them

Dry the wetsuits first

Diving finishes on the 24th and the cases go on the 25th. Damp neoprene shut in a case for a week arrives as a science experiment. Hang everything the moment you are off the boat on the 24th, give it the morning sun on the 25th, and pack it last.

Ship in the afternoon, not first thing

All six cases go to one counter

Narita has four separate Yamato counters, so this could have been fiddly — but it is not. Both your flights leave from Terminal 2: JL753 because JAL is oneworld, and AS124 because it is Hawaiian metal and Hawaiian departs Narita from Terminal 2 as a oneworld carrier too. So every case is addressed to the Terminal 2 counter, and the Seattle pair collect alongside everyone else rather than at a different building.

If the 1 November date is refused

Airport delivery may not accept a designation that far ahead — ask on the phone rather than finding out at the villa. First fallback: ship on the 24th for a 31 October arrival and let it wait a day or two at the counter. Second: send the cases to Art Hotel Aomori instead, since hotels hold luggage free, collect them on the 29th, and forward Aomori → Narita on the 31st. That is two shipments and about ¥44,000 — still nothing on a train.

Practicalities you will actually hit

Cash, cards, data, tax

Two of your four legs are rural, and rural Japan runs on rules that Tokyo has quietly moved on from. This is the short version of what bites people.

Why this shape

The coats never go south

The obvious plan is to carry everything to Miyakojima and post it onward. The better one is to notice that your winter clothes have no business on a tropical island — so they leave Tokyo on the 20th and go straight to Akita, while you fly south with swimwear.

That is what empties the 26th. Both sets of luggage are somewhere else that day, so the tightest connection of the trip — plane, monorail, Shinkansen, two cars in the dark — is done carrying nothing. It also means neither shipment ever sits on a paid shelf: each is timed to arrive where you will be.

Ask the inn to take Ship 1 in when you ring about the late check-in

Do this at the airport on day one

Get your passport stamped at Narita

Japan’s automated immigration gates are quick and they do not stamp your passport. Toyota’s own terms say that without the landing permission stamp they cannot confirm your date of entry — and without it they will not release the car. That is two bookings, ¥300,000 already paid, and both halves of the trip that depend on driving.

If you use the automated gate on 18 October, step aside afterwards and ask a staff member to stamp you in. It takes a moment and there is no downside. Everyone who might drive needs it — and since two cars means at least two drivers, that is not just one person’s job.

Also bring the passport itself to both rental desks, not a photo

The big one

Carry cash in the north

Tokyo and Miyakojima take cards nearly everywhere. Rural Akita and Aomori often do not — the small onsen, the samurai houses, the gorge car park, the family restaurants and the market stalls are frequently cash-only, and some are coins-only. Being caught short at Tsurunoyu with no change is a genuinely common way to miss a bath.

Assume cash from the 26th onward

How much

≈ ¥7,000 a day, each

A working rhythm for the northern legs: roughly ¥7,000 per person per day in cash covers food, baths, entries and the odd taxi. For six that is about ¥42,000 a day, so draw in decent chunks rather than daily — rural ATMs are not always where you need them.

≈ ¥300,000 cash for the group, 26 Oct – 2 Nov

Where to draw it

7-Eleven and Japan Post

Not every Japanese ATM takes foreign cards. The two that reliably do are 7-Eleven and Japan Post, both of which are everywhere including small towns. Draw before you leave Tokyo and top up in Aomori city — do not rely on finding one near Nyuto or Kakunodate.

Aomori city is your last easy top-up

Trains and shops

Suica or Pasmo, one each

Get one at Narita on the 18th and load ¥3,000–5,000. It covers every metro and bus gate you will touch, plus convenience stores, and removes fare-machine maths entirely. It works on your phone too if anyone would rather not carry a card.

Buy on arrival, day one

Data

eSIM beats pocket wifi for six

A pocket wifi router is one device the group has to stay within range of — which fails the moment you split on the 20th, or when four of you walk Dakigaeri and two drive. An eSIM each is usually cheaper and means everyone is independently navigable. Coverage is good even on the Hakkoda road; the weak spots are inside the gorges.

Set up before you fly

Shopping

Tax-free needs your passport

Over ¥5,000 in one shop on the same day, and you can claim the 10% consumption tax back at the till — but only with your actual passport, not a photo. Relevant for the kabazaiku tea caddies in Kakunodate and anything bought at Miyako’s distilleries.

Carry passports on the 27th

Driving

IDP, and an ETC card if offered

Every driver needs an International Driving Permit alongside the home licence — Japan will not accept the licence alone and desks do check. With two cars you need at least two IDPs, and realistically four so nobody is trapped as the only driver on a long day. Take the ETC card on both cars: it pays expressway tolls automatically and saves queueing at every barrier between Morioka and Aomori.

Two IDPs minimum · issued before you fly

Small things

Coins, towels, and closing days

Onsen day-use often wants exact coins and rarely provides a towel — bring a small one each or buy them at any convenience store. Museums in Japan close one weekday, usually Monday; check the closing day before any drive over thirty minutes, which on this trip means most of Aomori.

A ¥100-coin float is genuinely useful
