Kawaguchiko the view the day is built around

Getting there — drive it, and here is the logic

Self-drive from HachiojiRECOMMENDED

~2 h door to lake · ≈¥7,000 each

Marunouchi to Shinjuku, JR Chuo rapid to Hachioji (~50 min); Toyota Rent a Car is 3 min from the north exit, opens 08:00; one expressway straight to Kawaguchiko IC. Never touches central-Tokyo driving. The maths for six: van ≈¥22–28k + tolls ≈¥5k + fuel ≈¥3k + twelve local fares ≈¥9k = ≈¥40–45k total — against ≈¥50k just for six Fuji Excursion return tickets.

Private van charterZERO LOGISTICS

~2 h from the hotel door · ≈¥17–18k each

Alphard/HiAce with an English-speaking driver, pickup at Via Inn, can roll at 06:30 — Chureito by 08:30, before the coaches. Nobody drives, at ~2.5× the self-drive cost. Viator, KKday or GoWithGuide.

Train + rent at the lakeTHE FALLBACK

~2 h 20 · ≈¥12–13k each

Fuji Excursion 07:30 from Shinjuku (reserve ahead), then Toyota Rent a Car 5 min from Kawaguchiko station — that branch takes bookings by phone only. Keeps the famous train; starts you at Chureito an hour later. Use it if the Hachioji van is gone.

The highway bus (¥2,200) and the JR-via-Otsuki route exist, but a timetable is exactly what this day is better without.

Decide on the morning

This is the one day worth checking before you commit. The Fujigoko live webcams are pointed at the mountain from Kawaguchiko, and the forecast is usually honest about cloud. If Fuji is hidden, the day loses most of its point — switch to the Tokyo track and keep everyone’s money. The van makes that call cheap: Toyota rentals cancel free before pickup.

Worth knowing

The climb

Chureito’s 398 steps

Steep, and the reason some people skip it. There is a gently sloped path from the same car park to the same viewpoint without a single step — ask for the “slope route”.

Timing

Morning beats afternoon

Cloud builds around the summit as the ground warms, so the mountain is most reliably clear early. Chureito first, before the light flattens — at the pagoda by 11:00.

Autumn

Just before the peak

The Momiji Corridor’s maple festival normally starts early November, so late October catches it turning. The kokia at Oishi Park are red now, and colour rises with altitude.

Eat

Hoto, and Fujizakura beer

Hoto is the dish — nothing like udon despite appearances, exactly right after a cold morning at the lake. The local Fujizakura Kogen brewery does a very good weizen.

Group of six

Why the van wins

One van costs about half of six train tickets, and it is the only way to reach Motosuko and the quiet west-lake shores without a bus timetable. Hachioji pickup means you never drive a metre of central Tokyo.

If it is clear

Stay for sunset

Fuji turns pink for a few minutes just after the sun drops — the snow-lit version is “beni Fuji”. If the sky is clean at 16:00, push the return back an hour. You will not regret it.

Hard stop, by design

You fly at 11:50 on the 26th. Finishing today puts nearly three days between the last tank and the cabin — far past the 18-hour minimum, which is why the 24th and 25th are dry by design rather than by accident.

South on Route 103, the Hakkoda–Towada Gold Line. Leaving at 07:15 buys the pull-ins in low light and still puts you at the ropeway before it opens.

08:45

At the car park for the 09:00 first cabin — the single most valuable early start of the trip. The ropeway moves ~100,000 people in October and waits reach 90 minutes at peak foliage; be on the first cabin and you walk straight on.

Up top

Go long: the Mt Odake traverse, 4–5 hours — snow likely, it becomes a winter walk quickly. Take it easy: the 60-minute boardwalk loop straight off the ropeway — flat, marked, huge views. The summit peaks mid-October and works down, so the top may be frosted; the lower slopes and the road will still be spectacular.

PM

Tsuta-numa, the mirror pond — one of the most photographed autumn scenes in Japan; the short loop is level. Optional thirty minutes at Sukayu, the vast all-wood "thousand-person bath" — only if Tsurunoyu left anyone wanting a second.

19:00

31 Sat · October Osorezan's last day

Pick one: apples, beech forest, or the netherworld

Three genuinely different days, all worth doing, none combinable — and the day the group could split again. Let the sky pick: Hirosaki works in any weather; Shirakami and Osorezan need light.

Most rounded · any weather

Hirosaki & apples

The castle park mid-festival, Fujita Memorial Garden, the apple-pie trail (~50 bakery versions, mapped). If it's clear, add the Iwaki Skyline — 69 hairpins to the 8th station, nearly its last weekend of the season.

Shirakami & Juniko

Route 101 down the Sea of Japan coast, then Aoike's unreal cobalt in UNESCO beech forest at peak colour. The active four can take the Anmon Falls trail, ~3 hours; Senjojiki's rock shelf at sunset on the way home.

OUT 06:45 · ~2H EACH WAY

Closes tonight

Osorezan

One of Japan's three holiest sites: a sulphur-blasted crater of steaming vents, stacked stones and pinwheels on a startlingly blue caldera lake. Shut from 1 November until May — this is the only day it exists on your trip.
